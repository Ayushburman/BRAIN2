# Scientific Notes: How to Make Your Brain Faster, Improve Memory, Critical Thinking, and Achieve Difficult Goals

## 1. Sleep: The Ultimate Brain Enhancer

### What Science Says

During sleep, the brain:

* Consolidates memories
* Removes waste products via the glymphatic system
* Strengthens learning pathways
* Improves decision-making
* 

### Evidence

Research from the Harvard Medical School and National Institutes of Health shows sleep deprivation significantly reduces memory, attention, and cognitive performance.

### Protocol

* 7.5–9 hours sleep daily
* Consistent sleep/wake time
* No bright screens 1 hour before bed
* Cool, dark room

---


# 2. Exercise: The Brain Growth Drug

### What Science Says

Exercise increases:

* Blood flow to the brain
* BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)
* Neuroplasticity

BDNF acts like fertilizer for brain cells.

### Best Protocol

* 30–45 min brisk walking
* Running
* Cycling
* Swimming

3–5 times/week.


---

# 3. Spaced Repetition: The Memory Superpower

### Wrong Way

Read notes 10 times.

### Right Way

Recall information after increasing intervals:

* Day 1
* Day 3
* Day 7
* Day 14
* Day 30

### Why It Works

Each recall strengthens neural connections.

Used by:

* Medical students
* Polyglots
* Memory champions

Popular tool:
Anki

---

# 4. Active Recall

Instead of rereading:

### Close the Book

Ask:

* What did I just learn?
* Can I explain it from memory?

### Evidence

Active recall consistently outperforms passive reading in learning studies.

---

# 5. Deep Work

Concept popularized by:
Cal Newport

### Protocol

* 60–90 min uninterrupted work
* Phone away
* No notifications
* Single task only

Deep focus trains attention networks.

---

# 6. Learning Through Teaching

Known as the Feynman Technique.

Associated with:
Richard Feynman

### Method

Explain the concept as if teaching a 10-year-old.

If you cannot explain it simply, you don't understand it deeply.

---

# 7. Build Critical Thinking

### Ask 5 Questions

1. Is it true?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. Could there be another explanation?
4. What assumptions am I making?
5. What would prove me wrong?

This prevents cognitive biases.

# 12. The Impossible Task Formula

People often think a task is impossible because the brain fears uncertainty.

### Brain Hack

Replace:

> "Can I do this?"

with

> "What is the next tiny step?"

For example:

AIR 1 in GATE seems impossible.

Instead ask:

* Today's topic?
* Next 10 questions?
* Next 1 hour?

The brain handles small steps much better than huge goals.
